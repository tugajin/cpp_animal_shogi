# AlphaZero-style Self-Play Diversity — Literature Review

## Summary

**Verdict in one sentence:** The claim is *well-supported in its weak form and only partially supported in its strong form*. There is strong, convergent evidence — peer-reviewed analyses of AlphaZero's policy, practitioner measurements in shogi, and the design of every major successor system — that a single evolving AlphaZero-style policy **concentrates its self-play on a narrow, rapidly-stabilising subset of openings/lines and repeatedly generates similar (and in shogi, measurably often *identical*) early positions**. There is much weaker direct evidence that this concentration *measurably harms* final strength in chess/Go/shogi at AlphaZero scale; several sources argue the concentration is partly *optimal* (the plausible-move tree really is narrow) or even desirable. No published work found in this search directly measures "effective diversity" (e.g. clustering / near-duplicate rate of mid-game positions across a whole self-play corpus) for AlphaZero itself in Go or shogi — the shogi practitioner measurements below are the closest.

**Crucial distinction used throughout:**
- **(a) Exact-position repetition** — the same board position occurring many times.
- **(b) Similarity / clustering** — different but near-identical positions, same opening family, same pawn/castle structure, same strategic plan.
The researcher's suspicion is about (b), with (a) as a limiting case. Most evidence below is about (b) in the *opening* (first ~10–30 plies); evidence about (b) in the *middlegame* is thinner and mostly indirect (blind spots, adversarial exploits).

Type key: **[PR]** peer-reviewed paper · **[Pre]** preprint / conference submission · **[Book]** book · **[Prac]** practitioner documentation / blog / issue tracker / release notes.

## Findings

### 1. Direct analyses of self-play game diversity

#### 1.1 PNAS: Acquisition of chess knowledge in AlphaZero — the strongest quantitative source [PR]
- **Source:** McGrath, Kapishnikov, Tomašev, Pearce, Hassabis, Kim, Paquet & Kramnik, "Acquisition of chess knowledge in AlphaZero", *PNAS* 119(47) e2206625119, 14 Nov 2022. https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2206625119 — *verified live (opened, 2026-11-23)*.
- **What it measured (b, opening concentration):**
  - First-move prior entropy falls from **4.32 bits/move at random initialisation to 2.86 bits/move after 1M training steps** — i.e. AlphaZero *narrows* its options over training, the reverse of human history (humans went from 0.33 bits in 1400–1800 to 1.87 bits by late 20th century).
  - The narrowing is **abrupt**: between 25k–60k steps, the four replies 1…c5, c6, e5, e6 rise from 20% (their uniform share, 4 of 20 legal replies) to **80% of prior mass against 1.e4**; 2.♘f3 is settled on and 2.d4 "dropped almost as quickly" after 1.e4 e5.
  - **Within a run, collapse to one line:** after 1.e4 e5 2.♘f3 ♘c6 3.♗b5, individual trained networks put **92.8% / 88.9%** prior on 3…♘f6 (Berlin) in two seeds, and **89.2% / 85.8%** on 3…a6 in two other seeds — two moves humans consider near-equivalent. The preference is "developed rapidly and established early" (~100k steps for the Berlin). The authors explicitly note this contradicts the expectation of a uniform distribution.
  - Caveat the authors themselves add: self-play training games are "quick games enriched with stochasticity for boosting exploration", so the *prior* is not the self-play game distribution itself.
- **Counter-nuance in the same paper (see §4):** even after narrowing, AlphaZero's first-move entropy (2.86 bits) is *higher* than modern human GM play (1.87 bits).

#### 1.2 Tomašev et al.: opening diversity defined as prior entropy over the first 20 plies [PR/Pre]
- **Source:** Tomašev, Paquet, Hassabis, Kramnik et al., "Assessing Game Balance with AlphaZero: Exploring Alternative Rule Sets in Chess", arXiv:2009.04374 (2020), published version in *Communications of the ACM* 2022 as "Reimagining Chess with AlphaZero". https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.04374v1 and http://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2022/2/258230/fulltext?doi=10.1145%2F3460349 — *index*.
- **Claim/method:** §3.5 "Diversity" *defines* diversity as "the average information content (entropy) of the first T=20 plies of play from each variant's prior", explicitly because "the trained AlphaZero policy priors model the move probabilities of the positions in self-play training data, and reflects the statistics at which opening lines appear there." Entropy 0 = one forcing line; higher = "a more diverse set of middlegame positions". For classical chess they find AlphaZero "expresses a strong preference for the Berlin Defence" — i.e. classical chess scores *low* on this diversity measure relative to slower variants. This is DeepMind's own framing that self-play opening distribution is concentrated, and that the concentration can be quantified.

#### 1.3 Science paper's own opening table [PR]
- **Source:** Silver et al., "A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess, shogi, and Go through self-play", *Science* 362:1140–1144 (2018); arXiv version https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.01815v1 — *index*.
- Table 2 plots, for each of the 12 most popular human openings, "the proportion of self-play training games in which AlphaZero played each opening, against training time" — the curves show AlphaZero's self-play migrating over training toward a small subset of those openings and away from others. Secondary summaries of the released games (e.g. https://mpaluri.github.io/research-paper-summaries/papers/alphazero.html) state the final agent "almost never plays 1.e4, preferring 1.c4 and 1.d4". For shogi, the paper's released AlphaZero games / AobaZero replication (§5) show a strong preference for *aikakari* (相掛かり) and *kakugawari* (角換わり) bishop-exchange / wing-attack *ibisha* lines.

#### 1.4 "Game Changer" (Sadler & Regan, 2019) [Book]
- **Source:** Matthew Sadler & Natasha Regan, *Game Changer: AlphaZero's Groundbreaking Chess Strategies and the Promise of AI*, New in Chess, Jan 2019. The book itself was not read in full for this report; findings are via the publisher/DeepMind announcement https://deepmind.google/blog/alphazero-shedding-new-light-on-chess-shogi-and-go/ , the authors' ChessBase interview https://en.chessbase.com/post/interview-with-natasha-regan-and-matthew-sadler , and a chapter summary https://www.bookey.app/book/game-changer-by-matthew-sadler — *index only; treat exact wording with caution*.
- **What it documents:** Chapter 14, "AlphaZero's opening repertoire", is entirely devoted to the fact that AlphaZero, with no book, **self-developed a narrow, deliberate repertoire**: as White primarily **1.d4** (occasionally 1.♘f3, resembling Botvinnik's repertoire); as Black primarily **1…e5 with Berlin Defence structures** against 1.e4 and specific Ragozin-type setups against 1.d4. Sadler in the interview: AlphaZero "has also developed its own favourite openings and it has developed them really well". A further nuance reported from the book (Chess.com forum summary): DeepMind retrained AlphaZero multiple times and **different runs developed different opening preferences** (some 1.e4, some 1.c4/1.♘f3) — consistent with PNAS §1.1's seed-dependence: concentration *within* a run, arbitrariness *across* runs.
- **Interpretation:** This is (b) at the repertoire level, documented qualitatively by GMs who analysed "thousands of AlphaZero's chess games" (DeepMind). It is not a statistical diversity measurement.

#### 1.5 Diversifying AI: Towards Creative Chess with AlphaZero [Pre]
- **Source:** Zahavy, Veeriah, Hou, Waugh, Lai, Leurent, Tomašev, Schut, Hassabis & Singh (DeepMind), arXiv:2308.09175, v3 31 Jul 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09175?context=cs.AI / PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09175 — *index*.
- **Claim:** A *single* AlphaZero has systematic blind spots (notably the Penrose positions: plain AZ solves only ~3.6% of the Penrose set and 11.8% of the Challenge set at 1M simulations). The authors build AZ_db, a latent-conditioned *team* of AlphaZero players explicitly trained with **behavioural diversity** (intrinsic reward for visiting different board-and-action patterns — piece locations, castling rights) plus **response diversity** (PSRO matchmaking). Result: team members "prefer different moves in common openings", specialise in different openings, vary in castling / pawn structure; the team solves **twice as many** challenging puzzles as AZ and gains **+50 Elo** by selecting a player per opening. The paper's premise — that you must *add* a diversity objective because vanilla self-play does not produce it — is an implicit, DeepMind-authored acknowledgement of (b).

#### 1.6 Uncertainty-Guided Exploration for Efficient AlphaZero Training [PR — NeurIPS 2025]
- **Source:** Cheng, Tsai, Hong & Kandemir, NeurIPS 2025. https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/3a02da3fdfd592d9a5273101c3546611-Paper-Conference.pdf — *index*.
- **Claim:** "AlphaZero's self-play process remains inefficient due to **limited exploration of high-uncertainty positions**, the overlooked runner-up decisions in MCTS, and high variance in value labels." Only one MCTS decision per move is used; runner-ups are "discarded without exploration"; only ~40% of positions change decision after search. Branching from high-uncertainty positions with 10 variations/game improves sample efficiency **58.5% (9×9 Go, early) / 47.3% (19×19 Go, late)**. This is direct evidence that vanilla self-play trajectories *under-cover* the position space reachable from their own games.

#### 1.7 Adversarial policies vs KataGo — blind spots off the self-play distribution [PR]
- **Source:** Wang, Gleave et al., "Adversarial Policies Beat Superhuman Go AIs", ICML 2023, arXiv:2211.00241. https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00241 — *index*.
- **Claim:** An adversary using <14% of KataGo's training compute wins **>97–99%** against superhuman KataGo with a cyclic-group strategy that is weak in absolute terms (loses to human amateurs). Co-author Gleave (quoted in press coverage): "KataGo generalises well to many novel strategies, but it does get weaker the further away it gets from the games it saw during training" — i.e. self-play, however many millions of games, leaves large, systematically-unvisited regions of position space where the value/policy is wrong. Indirect but strong evidence for low *effective* coverage (b), in Go, for the best-engineered AlphaZero-style system.

#### 1.8 Lc0 testing folklore: without a book, games repeat [Prac]
- **Source:** Leela Chess Zero testing guide (GitHub wiki). https://github-wiki-see.page/m/LeelaChessZero/lc0/wiki/Testing-guide — *index*: "If you play engine matches without an opening book, **the engines will often play the same games over and over again**, which is obviously problematic." This is about *match* play (greedy, no noise), not training games — but it quantifies the underlying policy concentration that temperature/noise in training are fighting against.

### 2. Mitigations that implicitly acknowledge the problem

| System | Mitigation | Stated rationale | Type / Source |

| AlphaGo Zero / AlphaZero | **Temperature τ=1 for first 30 moves**, then τ→0 | Verbatim (AlphaGo Zero, *Nature* 2017, Methods): "For the first 30 moves of each game, the temperature is set to τ=1; this selects moves proportionally to their visit count in MCTS, and **ensures a diverse set of positions are encountered**. For the remainder of the game, an infinitesimal temperature is used." AlphaZero uses the same scheme (num_sampling_moves=30 in the published pseudocode). | [PR] https://www.lamda.nju.edu.cn/guolz/IntroAI/fall2025/readings/alphazero.pdf — *index* |
| AlphaGo Zero / AlphaZero | **Dirichlet noise at root**, P=(1−ε)p+εη, ε=0.25, α={0.3 chess, 0.15 shogi, 0.03 Go} | "this noise **ensures that all moves may be tried**, but the search may still overrule bad moves." ### 3. Population / league approaches — only where diversity/coverage is the stated motivation

- **Balduzzi et al., "Open-ended Learning in Symmetric Zero-sum Games", ICML 2019** [PR]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08106 — *index*. "If the game is approximately transitive, then self-play generates sequences of agents of increasing strength. However, nontransitive games… can exhibit strategic cycles" — self-play "cycles through agents without improving overall agent strength – simultaneously improving against one opponent and worsening against another." Their PSRO_rN uses "game-theoretic niching to **construct diverse populations of effective agents**, producing a stronger set of agents than existing algorithms." Motivation is *strategy-space coverage*, not position diversity per se — related but distinct; cite it as such.
- **Balduzzi, Tuyls, Perolat & Graepel, "Re-evaluating Evaluation", NeurIPS 2018** [PR]. https://export.arxiv.org/abs/1806.02643v2 — *index*. Nash averaging: evaluation (and by extension training) against a redundant / narrow set of agents is biased; intransitivity "has been linked to biodiversity in ecology, and may be useful when evolving populations of agents." Again: agent-diversity, not position-diversity.
- **Lanctot et al., PSRO, NeurIPS 2017** — framework generalising self-play/fictitious play/double oracle; naive self-play is the special case where the meta-strategy puts all weight on the *latest* policy. (Summarised in §3 sources above; original: "A Unified Game-Theoretic Approach to Multiagent Reinforcement Learning".)
- **AlphaStar league (Vinyals et al., *Nature* 575, 2019)** [PR, via secondary]. DeepMind's own summary, via https://gamesbeat.com/deepminds-alphastar-final-beats-99-8-of-human-starcraft-2-players/ — *index*: "Normally, AI agents engaged in self-play run the risk of **catastrophic forgetting**, in which they forget how to win against previous versions of themselves… This often kicks off a cycle in which the agents perceive valid strategies as less and less effective compared with a dominant strategy." Fix: fictitious self-play was "not robust enough" → league of main agents + main exploiters + league exploiters, with snapshots retained. Also note AlphaStar conditioned its policy on a human-data statistic *z* (build order) explicitly to preserve strategic diversity / counter mode collapse onto a single opening. StarCraft is not a board game and the failure mode is *forgetting/cycling*, not opening clustering — include only with that caveat.
- **dlshogi league (§5)** is the board-game instance of exactly this argument, made explicitly for shogi.

### 4. Counter-evidence

1. **PNAS's own numbers cut both ways** (§1.1): after full training AlphaZero's first-move entropy is 2.86 bits — *more* diverse than elite human play (1.87 bits). So "narrow" is true relative to the legal-move tree / uniform, and relative to what a *training corpus* ideally covers, but AlphaZero is not narrower than the human opening distribution at move 1. The concentration grows with depth (80% on 4 replies at move 1 for Black; ~90% on one move by move 3 in the Ruy Lopez example).
2. **YaneuraOu (YaneuraO, 2020) argues diversity is *harmful* and bias is good** [Prac, verified live]. https://yaneuraou.yaneu.com/2020/10/28/random-moves-in-a-self-play/ — Opening line: in shogi RL self-play "some device is necessary so that identical game records are not produced" (同一の棋譜ができてしまわないように何らかの工夫が必要) — listing (1) starting from random positions in floodgate games, (2) random book adoption, (3) random moves for the first few plies, (4) AlphaZero-style near-best random choice. But he then argues the popular belief is "exactly backwards": uniform random moves over-represent *furibisha* (swinging rook) — 6 of 30 first moves move the rook, so ≥50% of 6-random-move starts displace the rook — teaching "a wide variety of positions that will never occur in real play" wastes limited evaluation-function capacity. Floodgate-start data is better *because* it is biased toward similar, plausible positions: "「多種多様なものを学習すれば賢くなる」みたいな信念は人間の認知バイアスにすぎず" ("the belief that learning diverse things makes you smarter is just a human cognitive bias"). This is the strongest published practitioner counter-argument, and it concedes the premise (self-play needs an anti-duplication device) while disputing that more diversity = better.
3. **dlshogi duplicate-averaging experiment is mixed** (§5): averaging duplicate positions helped when fine-tuning a trained model (both policy and value loss better) but *hurt policy loss* when training from scratch — so exact-duplication harm is not cleanly demonstrated even by the practitioner who measured it.
4. **The null result of success:** AlphaZero, Leela Zero and KataGo all reached superhuman strength with the allegedly low-diversity data; no paper found shows an AlphaZero-style run *failing* or plateauing *because of* position correlation, with a controlled ablation (same compute, diversified vs vanilla self-play, board game, full scale). The Lc0 King's Gambit (+20 Elo, localised) and KataGo 9×9 (+~200 Elo, small board, forced-opening evaluation) results are the closest ablations, and both measure strength *in the diversified region*, not overall.
5. **Concentration may reflect the game, not the algorithm:** PNAS notes the two Ruy Lopez moves are near-equivalent yet each run picks one — but also that 1.e4/1.d4 dominance and the four main replies to 1.e4 match human theory. If the *optimal* opening distribution is itself concentrated, self-play concentration is partly correct learning, not pathology. Zahavy et al.'s diversity bonus likewise trades off against playing strength by design (player 0, with no diversity reward, is the control).

### 5. Shogi-specific (将棋)

This is where the most direct, quantitative practitioner evidence lives — in Japanese, mostly by dlshogi author Tadao Yamaoka.

#### 5.1 dlshogi: duplicate positions in self-play — measured [Prac, verified live]
- **Source:** Yamaoka, "将棋AIの実験ノート：重複局面の平均を学習" (Learning the average of duplicate positions), 17 Apr 2021. https://tadaoyamaoka.hatenablog.com/entry/2021/04/17/182108
- **Measurements (a — exact repetition, shogi!):**
  - With an initial-position set built from floodgate games up to ply 26 and default `--random 4` (Boltzmann selection for 4 plies), **33% of generated self-play positions were duplicates**.
  - With `--random 8` and 5M positions generated, **8.4% duplicates**.
  - His own production setup avoids the problem only by using an initial-position set of **~500 million positions**, with which "重複がほぼない" (duplicates almost never occur).
- **Mechanism (explicitly about (b)→(a)):** "30手目までが同一の場合、それ以降の手順も重複する可能性が高い（30手目以降もディリクレ分布によるノイズが加えられるが、同じになる確率の方が高い）" — if the first 30 plies coincide, the continuation is likely to coincide too, *despite* Dirichlet noise, because greedy selection dominates after ply 30. And: self-play position frequencies do not match real-game frequencies; "局面の偏りが大きすぎると学習に悪影響があると考えられる" (too much position bias harms learning); many duplicates were found to break training outright (policy loss → NaN, GitHub Issue #44).
- **Fix adopted:** aggregate duplicate positions into one sample, averaging policy distribution, outcome and value ("同一局面を平均化して学習") — see mixed results in §4.3.

#### 5.2 dlshogi: starting RL from the opening causes ~12% duplication — including in AobaZero data [Prac, verified live]
- **Source:** Yamaoka, "dlshogiが序盤に先手を過剰に評価することがある問題", 29 Nov 2020 (addenda 5/12 Dec 2020). https://tadaoyamaoka.hatenablog.com/entry/2020/11/29/182115
- **Measurements:** When dlshogi ran AlphaZero-style RL from opening initial positions, "**約12%が重複データになる**" (~12% of the data was duplicates). Checking AobaZero's preprocessed game records the same way: **11.643% duplicate positions** — code and figure given: `(len(hcpes)-len(np.unique(hcpes)))/len(hcpes) = 0.11643395063382735`.
- **Conclusion drawn by the author:** "序盤の局面から強化学習を行うと、データの偏りから序盤に偏って学習される懸念がある" (RL from opening positions risks learning biased toward the opening because of data bias) — so dlshogi **kept its diverse initial-position set** instead of switching to AlphaZero-style from-the-start self-play. Context: dlshogi's original initial set was mid/endgame-heavy, which caused its own opening-evaluation blind spot (overvaluing Sente at ply 7) — i.e. *both* extremes of the starting-position distribution produced measurable defects.
- **This directly answers the mission's premise:** in shogi exact positions *do* repeat at a ~1-in-8 to 1-in-3 rate in AlphaZero-style / short-randomisation self-play corpora, contrary to the assumption that they "rarely repeat" — the repetition is concentrated in the opening, exactly as the (b)-clustering mechanism predicts.

#### 5.3 dlshogi WCSC34 appeal document: diversity as an explicit design goal [Prac — tournament appeal paper]
- **Source:** Yamaoka, Kanō & Ōmori, "第34回世界コンピュータ将棋選手権 dlshogi with HEROZ アピール文章", WCSC34 (2024). https://www.apply.computer-shogi.org/wcsc34/appeal/appeal_round2_240503.pdf — *index*.
- Verbatim design claims: "強化学習時に初期局面集を使用して**局面の多様性を確保する**" (using an initial-position set during RL **to ensure position diversity**); §5.10 league play: "自分自身のプログラムのみで強化学習を行うと戦略に弱点が生まれる可能性がある。弱点をふさぐには多様なプログラムによるリーグ戦が有効" (RL with only one's own program can create strategic weaknesses; a league of diverse programs fixes them) — implemented by mixing existing programs into the league at 1/8 rate; §5.16: "自己対局では、序盤の同一の局面の教師データが多く生成される。それらの重複した局面を別のサンプルとして学習すると、モデルの学習に偏りが起きる" (in self-play, many teacher examples of identical opening positions are generated; training on them as separate samples biases learning). Also: self-play produced too few *nyūgyoku* (entering-king) wins, so Suishō self-play data for those was mixed in — a concrete coverage gap in a single policy's self-play distribution.

#### 5.4 AobaZero / Aoba-Komaochi (Yamashita, GPW 2021; CSA Journal Vol.32) [Prac/academic — GPW slides + CSA paper]
- **Sources:** Yamashita, "将棋の駒落ちの強化学習", GPW 2021 (online), 13 Nov 2021. http://www.yss-aya.com/komaochi/20211113gpw_komaochi.pdf — *verified live*. Yamashita, Hoki & Kobayashi, "AobaZeroの高速化と現在の状況", コンピュータ将棋協会誌 Vol.32 (2020/21). http://www.yss-aya.com/20210309aoba.pdf — *index*.
- **Findings:**
  - AlphaZero (shogi) is characterised as "相掛かりや角換わりが好きな居飛車党" (an *ibisha* player fond of aikakari and kakugawari); AobaZero confirmed "ほぼ同じ棋風" (almost the same playing style), ~150 Elo weaker.
  - GPW slides include a chart of the **proportion of unique (non-duplicate) positions at ply 30** by handicap: 2-piece and bishop handicaps have wide opening variation, while **lance handicap and even games (平手) look narrow** — with the gloss that in even games only aikakari/kakugawari can fight on equal terms, and in lance handicap the upper side is limited to *furibisha*.
  - For building a book (定跡) from self-play: taking the 800 most frequent games that are identical up to ply 16 in training, the even-game set was "**相掛かりや角換わりの類似形ばかり**" (nothing but similar aikakari/kakugawari forms, even after requiring the ply-16 piece placement to differ in ≥8 places) — and a book built from *human* games was judged better. This is (b) stated plainly by the AobaZero author: self-play games cluster into similar forms.
  - Also noted: raising temperature for the first 30 moves from 1.0 to 1.3 in the handicap project gave −12 Elo ("効果なし") — a small negative result on *more* opening randomisation.

#### 5.5 YaneuraOu teacher-data generation [Prac, verified live]
- See §4.2. The gensfen tradition in shogi NNUE (YaneuraOu) uses random moves / floodgate starts / books precisely to avoid identical records, but YaneuraO's considered position is that the *right* bias (toward positions that actually occur) beats diversity. Note the mission's premise ("random opening moves, stated reason = diversity") is therefore only half the story in shogi: the stated reason for randomisation is anti-duplication, and a leading practitioner publicly argues the diversity it creates is a *cost*, not a benefit.

## Verdict

**How well-supported is "AlphaZero-style self-play yields low-diversity training positions"?**

- **Opening concentration / clustering (b): Strongly supported.** Independent, convergent sources: DeepMind's own PNAS entropy measurements (4.32→2.86 bits; 80% mass on 4 replies; ~90% on a single 3rd move), DeepMind's chess-variants paper defining diversity by exactly this concentration, *Game Changer*'s repertoire analysis, Lc0's practitioner report that normal training "does not explore early game diversity broadly" (+20 Elo from fixing one opening family), and KataGo's release-note admission that empty-board self-play "will often severely lack opening diversity". Every major successor system ships an anti-concentration device (temperature, Dirichlet noise, books, fork games, varied komi/handicap/board, initial-position sets, leagues) — revealed-preference evidence that the problem is real.
- **Exact repetition (a) in shogi: Directly measured, and surprisingly high.** 33% duplicates (short randomisation), ~12% (dlshogi opening-start RL), 11.6% (AobaZero corpus). The researcher's assumption that exact positions rarely repeat in shogi is *false for the opening segment* of AlphaZero-style corpora, though presumably true for mid/late-game positions. No equivalent published measurement was found for chess or Go corpora.
- **Similarity beyond the opening / whole-corpus effective diversity: Indirectly supported only.** Evidence is blind-spot shaped: Penrose positions (chess), adversarial cyclic attack (Go), dlshogi's missing nyūgyoku wins and opening overvaluation, KataGo's benefit from external data for "situations that don't occur in self-play". No paper found computes a corpus-wide similarity/clustering statistic (e.g. near-duplicate rate under board-edit distance, or embedding-space coverage) for an AlphaZero-style run.
- **That the low diversity measurably harms training: Weakly-to-moderately supported.** Localised gains from diversification exist (Lc0 KG +20 Elo; KataGo 9×9 ~+200 Elo under diverse-opening evaluation; uncertainty-branching +47–58% sample efficiency in Go; AZ_db +50 Elo / 2× puzzles), but there is no controlled full-scale ablation, dlshogi's de-duplication results are mixed, and YaneuraO argues the opposite for NNUE-style learning. The safest formulation for the researcher: *single-policy self-play demonstrably concentrates on a narrow, self-reinforcing subset of lines and leaves identifiable regions of position space untrained; whether that concentration costs strength depends on whether evaluation/deployment visits those regions.*

## Could not verify
- *Game Changer* chapter 14 was not read in the original; its contents are reported from the authors' interview, DeepMind's announcement and a third-party chapter summary (index only).
- KataGo's *current* KataGoMethods.md was not opened directly; varied-settings evidence comes from the 2019 paper, the public training config, the README and the 9×9 release notes (index).
- AlphaStar claims are from DeepMind's *Nature* paper as reported by secondary coverage, not the Nature text itself.
- No source was found that directly measures diversity/similarity of *AlphaZero's own shogi or Go* self-play corpora (all shogi measurements are dlshogi/AobaZero; all Go evidence is KataGo/Leela Zero or adversarial-robustness work).
- No dedicated "self-play data correlation" statistics (e.g. autocorrelation of positions within/across games, effective sample size of a replay buffer) were found in the board-game literature searched, in English or Japanese.
