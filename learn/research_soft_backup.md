# Soft Backup Operators in Game-Tree Search and Value-Function Learning — Prior Art

## Summary

**Bottom line for the dobutsu-shogi design (a) + (b):**

1. **The operator itself is old and well-named in RL, but the exact combination proposed — hard-minimax (UBFM-style) search for play / tree generation, with training targets *recomputed offline* by a softmax-weighted backup over the recorded tree, for a value-only network — has, in the sources found, essentially one direct precedent, and it is in shogi: Monte Carlo Softmax Search (MCSS).** Igarashi, Morioka & Yamamoto, *Learning Position Evaluation Functions Used in Monte Carlo Softmax Search* (arXiv:1901.10706, 2019; building on Kirii et al., GPW 2017) define exactly a Boltzmann-weighted-average backup (with a soft-*min* for the opponent), derive gradients of the root/node value w.r.t. the leaf evaluation parameters through that backup — i.e. proposal (b) — and combine it with TD(λ), Q-learning, regression and search-bootstrapping losses — i.e. proposal (a) in spirit. Crucially: **that paper contains no experiments**; implementation/evaluation is stated as future work. No measured playing-strength effect of MCSS soft backup was found.
2. **No soft-minimax / softmax-backup use was found in AlphaZero, MuZero, KataGo, Leela Chess Zero, AobaZero, dlshogi, or elmo** (details §2). Those systems either (i) use MCTS *mean* (visit-weighted average) backup — which is already a soft, non-minimax backup, but a different one — with the value target = game outcome `z` (AlphaZero/Lc0/AobaZero/dlshogi-RL), or blended with the MCTS root value (KataGo, dlshogi, "soft-Z"), or (ii) use hard alpha-beta with supervised/Bonanza-style learning (elmo). Athénan/UBFM — the closest published analogue of the team's search — uses **hard** minimax backup plus tree bootstrapping, not soft backup.
3. **The strongest empirical evidence for soft backup is outside board games**: in deep Q-learning on Atari, replacing max by the Boltzmann (softmax-weighted-average) operator in the *target* reduced overestimation and gradient noise and often beat (double) DQN (Song, Parr & Carin, ICML 2019). Mellowmax and G-learning give convergent / bias-reducing variants in smaller domains. **The strongest board-game evidence points partly the other way**: Willemsen, Baier & Kaisers (2021/22) show that in AlphaZero, *hardening* the value target (greedy backup, A0GB) beats softer, exploration-contaminated targets (game outcome, MCTS mean "soft-Z", A0C) on Connect-Four and Breakthrough. Softening a target and removing exploration bias are different axes — do not conflate them.
4. **Established drawbacks, all directly relevant at value scale [-1,1]**: (i) value-semantics drift — a soft-backup fixed point is the value of a *stochastic* (Boltzmann/entropy-regularised) policy, not the game-theoretic minimax value; MCSS itself notes its finite-temperature PV need not coincide with the minimax PV; (ii) compounding with depth — softness is applied at every ply, so root targets drift further from minimax the deeper the tree, and the opponent's soft-min systematically assumes a sub-optimal opponent; (iii) the Boltzmann weighted-average operator is **not a non-expansion/contraction for all temperatures** (Littman 1996, cited by Asadi & Littman and by Song et al.) — mellowmax/log-sum-exp variants were invented largely to fix this; (iv) temperature is scale-dependent and tuning-sensitive — no paper gives a universal τ for [-1,1] game values (guidance in §5 is partly derived, and labelled as such).

Confidence: high on the RL operator taxonomy and on TDLeaf; high on the *absence* claim for the named AlphaZero-family/shogi systems (based on their defining papers/docs — absence of evidence, stated as "no prior art found", not proof of non-existence); medium on MCSS details (single source, read in full).

## Findings

### 1. Named soft-backup variants: definitions, formulas, motivation

Notation: children values `x_1…x_n` (for a MAX node; MIN nodes use the negated form), temperature `τ > 0`, inverse temperature `β = 1/τ` (Asadi & Littman write `ω` for β). All variants → hard max as `τ→0 (β→∞)` and → mean as `τ→∞ (β→0)` (for the weighted-average and mellowmax forms).

| Variant | Formula | Defining / canonical source | Key property |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boltzmann / softmax weighted average** ("boltz", softmax Bellman operator) | `boltz_τ(x) = Σ_i x_i · e^{x_i/τ} / Σ_j e^{x_j/τ}` | Used in Boltzmann exploration (Sutton & Barto); analysed as a Bellman operator by Littman (1996 thesis, cited in both papers below); Asadi & Littman, *An Alternative Softmax Operator for Reinforcement Learning*, ICML 2017; Song, Parr & Carin, *Revisiting the Softmax Bellman Operator*, ICML 2019 | `mean ≤ boltz ≤ max`. Differentiable. **Not a non-expansion for some τ** → can have multiple fixed points / misbehave in learning and planning (Asadi & Littman 2017). This is the operator in the team's proposal (a) as described ("softmax-weighted average"). |
| **Log-sum-exp (LSE) soft max** ("soft value" of maximum-entropy RL / Soft Q-Learning) | `LSE_τ(x) = τ · log Σ_i e^{x_i/τ}` | Todorov's linearly-solvable MDPs (lineage cited by Fox et al.); Haarnoja et al., *Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies* (Soft Q-Learning), ICML 2017: `V*_soft(s) = α log ∫ exp(Q*_soft(s,a')/α) da'`; Nachum, Norouzi, Xu & Schuurmans, *Bridging the Gap Between Value and Policy Based RL* (PCL), NeurIPS 2017: `V*(s) = τ log Σ_a exp((r+γV*(s'))/τ)`; Haarnoja et al., Soft Actor-Critic, 2018 | `max ≤ LSE ≤ max + τ·log n`. It is the exact value of the entropy-regularised objective `E[r] + τ·H(π)`, whose optimal policy is Boltzmann: `π*(a|s) ∝ exp(Q(s,a)/τ)`. Overshoots max by up to `τ log n` — with large branching factor (shogi/dobutsu drops) this term is not negligible. |
| **Mellowmax** | `mm_ω(x) = (1/ω) · log( (1/n) Σ_i e^{ω x_i} ) = LSE_{1/ω}(x) − (log n)/ω` | Asadi & Littman, *An Alternative Softmax Operator for Reinforcement Learning*, ICML 2017, PMLR 70 | `mean ≤ mm ≤ max` (like boltz, it never overshoots max). **A non-expansion w.r.t. ∞-norm for all ω** → unique fixed point, convergent generalised value iteration; differentiable; entropy-regularisation interpretation. Follow-ups: Kim, Asadi, Littman & Konidaris, *DeepMellow* / Mellowmax-DQN (AAMAS 2019 extended abstract; full paper 2019) — removing the target network in DQN. If the team wants an LSE-flavoured backup, mellowmax is the version designed to keep game-value semantics (bounded by max). |
| **Maximum-entropy / SAC soft backup** | `V(s) = E_{a∼π}[ Q(s,a) − α log π(a|s) ]`, with soft Bellman backup `Q ← r + γ E[V(s')]` | Haarnoja et al., *Soft Actor-Critic*, ICML 2018 (and 2017 Soft Q-Learning above) | Not a pure function of child values: it is an expectation under the *current policy* plus an entropy bonus. Value semantics are explicitly "return + entropy", i.e. drift from game value is by design. |
| **G-learning soft update** | Backup replaces max by a KL-regularised (to a prior policy) soft optimum; in cost form a log-sum-exp over actions weighted by the prior | Fox, Pakman & Tishby, *Taming the Noise in Reinforcement Learning via Soft Updates*, UAI 2016 | Motivation is explicitly the winner's-curse / max-bias below; the regularisation coefficient is *scheduled* away as data accumulates (§5). Operator proved a contraction in their formulation. |
| **Soft minimax / Monte Carlo Softmax Search (MCSS) backup** | MAX node: `V(s) = Σ_a P(a;s)·Q(s,a)`, `P(a;s) = e^{Q(s,a)/T_a}/Σ_x e^{Q(s,x)/T_a}`; MIN (opponent) node: `P_b(b;v) = e^{−Q_b(v,b)/T_b}/Σ_x e^{−Q_b(v,x)/T_b}` | Kirii, Hara, Igarashi, Morioka & Yamamoto, *Stochastic Selective Search Applied to Shogi*, GPW 2017 (in Japanese), as presented/extended in Igarashi, Morioka & Yamamoto, arXiv:1901.10706 (2019) | The two-player, alternating form of the Boltzmann weighted average. Exactly the team's proposed operator applied inside a shogi search tree (§2, §3). |
| **ε-soft / ε-max** (minor) | `(1−ε)·max(x) + ε·mean(x)` | Listed as a comparator operator in Asadi & Littman 2017 | Non-expansion, but non-differentiable and "starves" non-max actions less smoothly; mainly a baseline. |
| **Product propagation** (historical ancestor, not softmax) | Back up win *probabilities* by product rules: at a MAX node `1 − Π_i (1 − p_i)`, at MIN node `Π_i p_i` | Pearl (1981/82); Tzeng & Purdom, *A Theory of Game Trees*, AAAI 1983 | Motivated by minimax *pathology* (Nau): if leaf values are probabilities of a forced win and sibling subpositions are independent, minimax discards information (a node with 100 independent 10%-win moves is almost surely won, but minimax values it at 0.1). An early proof that "hard max/min is the wrong backup for *uncertain* leaf values" — the conceptual ancestor of all soft backups in game trees. Baum (AAAI 1992) qualifies it: near the leaves a product-like rule is better, near the root minimax is approximately correct — an early statement of depth-dependent softness. |

**Motivation — maximisation bias (the canonical citation).** Thrun & Schwartz, *Issues in Using Function Approximation for Reinforcement Learning*, Proc. 4th Connectionist Models Summer School, 1993: function approximators induce (generalisation) noise on value estimates; taking a max over noisy estimates **systematically overestimates** the true value (`E[max_i (v_i + noise_i)] ≥ max_i v_i`), and they derive conditions under which this makes Q-learning fail, plus bounds on required approximator accuracy / discount. This is the result cited for overestimation bias by essentially the whole later literature (e.g. Fujimoto et al., TD3, 2018; Lan et al., Maxmin Q-learning, 2020, both citing Thrun & Schwartz 1993 as the first investigation). Fox et al. (2016) restate it as the "winner's curse". Soft backups attack it by down-weighting the single argmax child: Song et al. (2019) prove/show the softmax operator can *reduce overestimation error and gradient noise* relative to max. Note the asymmetry for two-player trees (derived, not from a single paper): at MAX nodes max-bias inflates values, at MIN nodes min-bias deflates them, so in a minimax tree the bias does not cancel in any simple way — softening both sides (as MCSS does) shrinks backed-up values toward the sibling mean at every ply.

Sources (this section): Asadi & Littman 2017, http://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/asadi17a/asadi17a.pdf (index, read 2026-11-23); Song et al. 2019, http://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/song19c.html and https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00456v1 (index); Thrun & Schwartz 1993, https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub1/thrun_sebastian_1993_1/thrun_sebastian_1993_1.pdf (index); Fox et al. 2016, https://royf.org/pub/pdf/Fox2016Glearning.pdf (index); Nachum et al. 2017, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/abs/1702.08892 (index); Haarnoja et al. 2017 via https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/1702.08165v2 (index); Tzeng & Purdom 1983, https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1983/AAAI83-080.pdf (index); MCSS paper, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.10706 (**verified — full text read**, 2026-11-23).

### 2. Soft backup in board-game self-play learning

**Direct hit — shogi, MCSS (the only system found that does what the team proposes).**
Igarashi, Morioka & Yamamoto (2019), building on Kirii et al. (GPW 2017; the "Shibaura Shogi Softmax" team used MCSS at the 27th World Computer Shogi Championship, 2017):

- **Search**: selective search (like MCTS, but leaves evaluated by a positional evaluation function + quiescence search, not playouts). Boltzmann distributions are used *both* for stochastic node selection and for backup.
- **Backup (proposal (a), inside the search)**: node value = expectation of child values under the Boltzmann backup policy above; opponent nodes use `exp(−Q/T_b)`. As `T→0` it reduces to minimax.
- **Learning (proposal (b))**: because every node value is a *continuous* function of the evaluation parameters ω, they derive `∇_ω V(root)` and `∇_ω Q` recursively through the backup (their Eqs. 5.1–5.4): a leaf's gradient is weighted by the product of backup probabilities along the path — precisely "soft backup distributes gradient to all leaves, weighted by backup probability, instead of only the PV leaf". They plug these gradients into supervised (Bonanza-style/KL) learning, TD(λ), Q-learning, policy-gradient (PGLeaf lineage), regression-to-game-result, and Veness-style search bootstrapping with target `H(s) ≈ V_soft(s)` at internal nodes.
- **Known problem they themselves flag**: with a single Boltzmann function for selection and backup, "the optimal sequence of moves estimated by MCSS with a finite temperature parameter did not always conform to the principal variation defined in the minimax search". Their fix: **decouple selection policy from backup policy, and use a low temperature for the backup policy** to recover the minimax PV. Also: "we can use a minimax operator or set T at a small value close to zero when no learning is being done" — i.e. soft backup is for *learning*, hard minimax for *play*. This is exactly the team's split (hard search, soft targets), arrived at independently in the shogi literature.
- **Measured effect: none published in this paper.** The conclusion states implementation into a shogi program and evaluation are future work. A 2022 JSAI follow-up (Igarashi group) extends the framework beyond two-player games and tests on a maze example — no shogi playing-strength result found. **So: the closest prior art is a formalism + learning-rule derivation with no empirical validation.** The team's experiment would, as far as found, be the first measured test of this idea in shogi-like games.

**The AlphaZero family — no soft minimax; mean backup + outcome targets.**

- **AlphaZero** (Silver et al., 2017/18, chess/shogi/Go): MCTS backup is the *mean* of leaf values over visits along the trajectory (standard MCTS averaging); the value network is trained to the **game outcome z** from self-play, the policy to visit counts. No softmax/minimax backup anywhere. Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.01815v1 (index).
- **MuZero / Reanalyse** (Schrittwieser et al. 2020; Schrittwieser et al., *Online and Offline RL by Planning with a Learned Model*, NeurIPS 2021): same MCTS mean backup; Reanalyse *recomputes targets from stored trajectories with the current model + fresh MCTS* — conceptually adjacent to the team's "recompute targets from recorded trees", but the recomputed target is the MCTS (mean-backup) root value / n-step return, not a soft-minimax recomputation. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.06294 (index).
- **KataGo** (Wu, *Accelerating Self-Play Learning in Go*, 2019): AlphaZero-style mean backup; value training uses game outcome **plus TD-like targets derived from the MCTS root value estimate** at two λ scales (documented in the KataGo training-data format), plus playout-cap randomisation and policy-target pruning. No soft minimax. Sources: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.10565 ; https://katagoarchive.org/g170/selfplay/README.txt (index).
- **Leela Chess Zero / Leela Zero**: PUCT MCTS, mean backup, value target = game outcome. No soft backup found. Source: https://github-wiki-see.page/m/LeelaChessZero/lc0/wiki/Technical-Explanation-of-Leela-Chess-Zero (index).
- **AobaZero** (shogi, AlphaZero reproduction): MCTS (PUCT) with visit-count policy targets and game-result value targets recorded in extended CSA records; no soft backup found. Source: https://deepwiki.com/kobanium/aobazero/5-training-pipeline (index — secondary summary of the public codebase).
- **dlshogi** (Yamaoka): UCT MCTS self-play; training loss mixes policy cross-entropy, **result loss (game outcome) and a "value loss" against the search's value estimate** (`val_lambda`, default ≈0.333 in the documented config) — again a mean-backup MCTS value, not a soft-minimax value. Supervised mode uses teacher positions from elmo-style search data. No soft backup found. Source: https://deepwiki.com/TadaoYamaoka/DeepLearningShogi/5.3-reinforcement-learning (index — secondary summary).
- **elmo** (shogi, WCSC27 champion, AlphaZero's opponent): hard alpha-beta search; evaluation learned by supervised (Bonanza-method-style) optimisation on professional/self-play records. No neural value-only soft backup; not an AlphaZero-style system at all. (Background from AlphaZero paper's description of elmo as an alpha-beta program, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.01815v1 .)

**Other board-game backup-target work — the counter-current.**

- **Willemsen, Baier & Kaisers, *Value targets in off-policy AlphaZero: a new greedy backup?*, Neural Computing & Applications 34:1801–1814 (2021/22), https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/30870/ , DOI 10.1007/s00521-021-05928-5.** Defines a family of AlphaZero value targets: original (game outcome z, on-policy, exploration-contaminated), **soft-Z** (= MCTS root value `v̂_MCTS`, i.e. the mean-backup value; name from Carlsson), A0C, and their **A0GB greedy backup** (follow the greedy/max-visit path to a leaf, use its network value). Results: A0GB finds the optimal policy in a small tabular domain where original AlphaZero fails; soft-Z, A0C and A0GB all beat the original target on **Connect-Four and Breakthrough**, with the greedy (hardest) target the proposed winner. Their diagnosis: targets that average over exploratory moves teach the value of the *exploratory* policy, not of greedy play. Implication for the team: a softmax-weighted target is, like soft-Z, a target for a *stochastic* policy's value — expect the same class of mismatch if play is hard minimax, unless τ is small.
- **MCTS backup-rule literature (planning, not training targets)**: standard MCTS averaging converges slowly in minimax-type trees / tactical traps; alternatives studied include Coulom's maximum backup, implicit minimax backups (Lanctot et al., CIG 2014 — stronger play in Kalah, Breakthrough, Lines of Action), and *Bayesian Optimisation for Backpropagation in MCTS* (2020, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09325 ) proposing Softmax MCTS and Monotone MCTS backups that outperformed prior backups in computer Go with a deep value net. Direction of this literature: moving MCTS backup *toward* minimax (max), not away from it — again opposite to softening a minimax backup, and a warning that in tactical domains averaging-type backups are known to be slow to propagate a newly found forced win/loss.

**Explicit negative finding:** no published system was found that (i) trains a value network on **soft-minimax targets recomputed from recorded hard-minimax trees** (team's (a) exactly), other than the MCSS formalism above where the soft backup lives inside the search and no results are reported; nor (ii) any use of soft backup in **dobutsu shogi** or other small animal-shogi variants. Searches in English for soft backup + shogi/chess/Go engines returned only MCSS and the RL literature above.

### 3. TDLeaf and through-search / differentiable backup

**Correction to the mission's attribution (established):** TDLeaf(λ) was coined by **Baxter, Tridgell & Weaver** (KnightCap), not Tesauro. Tesauro's contribution is TD-Gammon (TD(λ) + 1-ply search in backgammon, 1992–95), the precursor that Baxter et al. set out to generalise to deep-search games. Beal & Smith (1997) independently had the same idea for piece values (noted in the Chess Programming Wiki summary).

**Definition.** Baxter, Tridgell & Weaver, *TDLeaf(λ): Combining Temporal Difference Learning with Game-Tree Search*, arXiv:cs/9901001 (1999; journal version *Learning to Play Chess Using Temporal Differences*, Machine Learning 2001), and *KnightCap: A Chess Program that Learns by Combining TD(λ) with Game-Tree Search*, arXiv:cs/9901002 / ICML 1998:

- Run minimax/alpha-beta search from the root position at time t; take the **leaf of the principal variation** (the "principal leaf"); form the TD(λ) temporal-difference sequence over the *principal leaves of successive positions in the actual game* (t, t+1, …), and update the evaluation parameters at those leaves: the root's backed-up value is, by minimax, exactly the principal leaf's evaluation, so `∂(root value)/∂ω = ∂(principal-leaf eval)/∂ω` and all other leaves get zero gradient.
- **KnightCap result**: improved from ≈1650 to ≈2100 (arXiv:cs/9901001 abstract) / 2150 (cs/9901002 and journal version — the two versions differ; report both) Elo on FICS in 308 games / 3 days, playing **online against humans, not self-play**.

**Known properties / limitations (published critiques).**

1. **One node per search.** Veness, Silver, Uther & Blair, *Bootstrapping from Game Tree Search*, NeurIPS 2009: "Samuel's approach and TD-Leaf suffer from three main drawbacks. First, they only update one node after each search, which discards most of the information contained in the search tree. Second, their updates are based purely on positions that have actually occurred in the game, or which lie on the computed line of best play [which] may not be representative of the wide variety of positions that must be evaluated…" Their remedies: **RootStrap** (train root eval toward the same search's minimax value) and **TreeStrap** (train *every* node in the tree toward its minimax backed-up value, all from a single search at the same time step) — implemented in the chess program Meep (linear eval), which reached master-level play online from random initial weights by self-play, which KnightCap-from-random-weights had not achieved (Veness et al. note KnightCap's strong result relied on expert-initialised material values and a controlled online regime; TDLeaf-from-random in chess was disappointing).
2. **Discontinuity / PV switching.** The minimax root value is a piecewise function of ω: an arbitrarily small parameter change can switch the PV, so the TDLeaf gradient is the gradient of one leaf almost everywhere and undefined at switch points; credit is assigned to exactly one line. This is precisely the defect MCSS's soft backup is designed to remove (Igarashi et al.: "minimax calculation complicates propagating the values of the gradient vectors toward the root node. Even a small variance of the parameters… discontinuously changes a PV's path and the derivatives of the root node's value… a softmax operator makes it possible to express the values of all the nodes as continuous functions of the parameters").
3. **Bootstrapping horizon**: TDLeaf's target comes from a search at a *later* game position, coupling learning to the opponent's actual moves; TreeStrap/Athénan-style tree learning removes that by using the same search (Veness et al. 2009; Cohen-Solal, *Learning to Play Two-Player Perfect-Information Games without Knowledge*, arXiv:2008.01188 / JMLR version — generalises tree bootstrapping to non-linear (neural) evaluation inside UBFM/Descent, "no information is lost", and beats ExIt on Hex/Othello/Arimaa; still **hard** minimax backup).

**Published combinations of TDLeaf-style through-search training with soft/differentiable backup — what exists:**

- **MCSS (above, §2)** is the direct published combination: TD(λ) (and Q-learning, regression, bootstrapping) with gradients taken *through a Boltzmann-soft backup*, distributing over all leaves. Formalism only, no experiments. This is, as far as found, **the** prior art for the team's proposal (b).
- **PGLeaf / policy-gradient through search** (Igarashi group, cited within MCSS paper): policy-gradient expectation method over the softmax search tree, with PGLeaf as its PV-only fast approximation — i.e. the hard/soft pair (PGLeaf ≈ TDLeaf-style PV-only; full expectation = soft-backup version) already exists in that lineage.
- **Differentiable planning in single-agent RL** (adjacent, not two-player): Tamar et al., *Value Iteration Networks*, NeurIPS 2016 — a differentiable approximation of value iteration (max replaced by a differentiable planning module) trained end-to-end by backprop; Nachum et al. PCL (2017) and Haarnoja Soft Q-Learning — soft Bellman backups trained by gradient descent throughout. These establish that soft backups are trainable end-to-end at scale, but in MDP (single-agent, expectation-over-chance) settings, not adversarial trees.
- **Modern "learning through search" with hard backup**: Expert Iteration (ExIt, Anthony et al. 2017), AlphaZero/MuZero Reanalyse, Athénan tree learning — all differentiate *around* the search (distil its output as a target) rather than *through* it, and all keep the backup hard (minimax) or mean (MCTS). **No published work was found that backpropagates through a soft-minimax backup in a modern neural (AlphaZero-era) board-game system, and none for UBFM/Descent specifically.** Giraffe (Lai, 2015) is sometimes cited in this lineage: it used TDLeaf-style learning with a neural evaluation in chess — with hard minimax.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/9901001 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/9901002 ; https://www.gambit.se/chess-research/bearbetat-material/baxter-tridgell-weaver_1998_knightcap-temporal-difference-learning.pdf ; Veness et al. http://blair.web.cse.unsw.edu.au/pubs/2009VenessSilverUtherBlairNIPS.pdf ; Cohen-Solal https://arxiv.org/html/2008.01188v5 ; VIN https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/hash/c21002f464c5fc5bee3b98ced83963b8-Abstract.html (all index except MCSS, read in full).

### 4. Empirical evidence: benefits, negative results, drawbacks

**Positive (established, non-board-game RL):**

- **Song, Parr & Carin (ICML 2019)**: DQN and DDQN with the max in the *target network* replaced by the Boltzmann softmax operator, on several ALE/Atari games: "variants using the softmax Bellman operator can achieve higher test scores, and reduce the Q-value overestimation as well as the gradient noise on most of them", in some cases beating DDQN — despite the operator's fixed point being sub-optimal for the true objective. They also prove convergence (in their sense) to the standard Bellman operator exponentially fast in β, and bound the deviation of the soft Q-function from optimal.
- **Asadi & Littman (ICML 2017)**: mellowmax-SARSA (MM-SARSA, with state-dependent temperature) converges (tabular) and "performs favourably in practice" in their domains; DeepMellow (Kim et al. 2019): DQN + mellowmax *without* a target network outperformed DQN-without-target-network and learned faster than DQN with one, on Acrobot/LunarLander/Breakout/Seaquest (small domains).
- **Fox et al. (UAI 2016)**: G-learning's soft update reduced value-estimation bias and sped convergence to optimal value/policy in their (small, noisy) examples.
- **Maximum-entropy RL (Soft Q-Learning / SAC)**: strong, replicated results in continuous control — but note this changes the *objective* (reward + entropy), so it is evidence that soft-backup training is stable and effective, not that soft values approximate game values well.

**Negative / cautionary (established):**

- **Non-contraction of the Boltzmann operator.** Asadi & Littman: boltz "is not a non-expansion… prone to misbehaviour" (multiple fixed points possible); Song et al. repeat Littman (1996): softmax Bellman operator "is not a contraction, for certain temperature parameters". The team's proposal (a) uses the operator only to *compute regression targets offline* (not as an iterated Bellman operator), which sidesteps the fixed-point pathology for target computation — but if the network is then used inside the same soft backup repeatedly across training generations, the semantics still iterate. (This mitigation is analysis, not a published result.)
- **Board-game direction is opposite in the AlphaZero target literature** (Willemsen et al., §2): soft/mean targets (soft-Z) helped vs outcome targets, but the *greedy* target helped most; and MCTS-backup literature (implicit minimax, max backup, Softmax/Monotone MCTS in Go) generally moves *away* from averaging toward minimax for tactical correctness. No board-game experiment found shows a softmax-weighted minimax target beating a hard minimax target. **That specific comparison appears untested in print** — the MCSS paper proposes it and reports no results.
- **Value-semantics drift (established in theory, quantified in MCSS's own caveat)**: the soft-backup value is the expected value under Boltzmann play by both sides. With hard-minimax play at deployment, the network is trained to predict the outcome of a *different* (stochastic, weaker) pair of policies. LSE form additionally inflates values by up to `τ·log(branching)` per ply. MCSS: finite-T optimal line ≠ minimax PV. Pearl/Baum lineage: averaging-type backups are defensible near the frontier (where leaf values are uncertain) and minimax near the root — soft targets therefore distort most exactly where the root decision is made.
- **Compounding with depth (derived from the definitions; consistent with the above)**: soft backup applied at every ply of a depth-D tree composes D soft operators. Each ply pulls the value toward the sibling mean (boltz form) — a forced win behind one refutation line and a forced loss behind another both get averaged away, so deep tactical values are systematically *compressed toward the local mean*, more so the deeper the tree. No paper found measures this compounding in a minimax tree; flagging it as a reasoned prediction, not an established result. A depth-decaying temperature (soft near leaves, hard near root, per Baum 1992's qualitative conclusion) is the literature-consistent mitigation — untested for this use.
- **Temperature sensitivity**: Hu, Abbeel & Fox, *Count-Based Temperature Scheduling for Maximum Entropy RL* (NeurIPS 2021 workshop): most MaxEnt methods use constant temperature "contrary to intuition"; SQL with a badly chosen fixed temperature can underperform; state/count-dependent scheduling helped on Atari. StaTeS-SQL thesis work similarly reports fixed-temperature SQL "can under-perform a random policy if the temperature is not selected properly" on some games. Expect a τ sweep to be necessary, and the optimum to move as the network's error scale shrinks during training.

### 5. Practical guidance on temperature choice

**What the literature actually gives (established):**

1. **τ is in value units — it only has meaning relative to the scale of sibling value gaps and of evaluation noise.** All sources parameterise by β=1/τ multiplying values (Asadi & Littman's ω, Haarnoja's α, MCSS's T_a/T_b). Rescaling values by k is equivalent to rescaling τ by k. There is no scale-free default.
2. **Anneal / schedule it, don't fix it.** Fox et al. (2016): start soft (regularisation strong when estimates are noisy / data scarce), decrease the penalty — i.e. **τ ↓ (β ↑) as learning proceeds**. Hu et al. (2021): same direction, made state-dependent via visit counts (τ high where/when confidence is low). MCSS: use a **low backup temperature** specifically so the backed-up values/PV stay close to minimax, with freedom to use a different (higher) temperature for *selection/exploration*. Separating "backup τ" from "exploration τ" is an explicit published recommendation (MCSS).
3. **Auto-tuning exists in MaxEnt RL**: SAC (Haarnoja et al. 2018, and the 2019 auto-tuned version) adjusts α to hold the policy at a target entropy — i.e. τ is chosen so the *induced Boltzmann distribution* has a specified spread, rather than by guessing a value-scale number. The analogue for the team: pick τ to achieve a target effective number of children / entropy of the backup weights at a typical node, measured on real trees.
4. **Mellowmax if you want LSE geometry without overshoot**: its `−τ log n` correction keeps the result ≤ max for any n, removing the branching-factor-dependent inflation of plain LSE (Asadi & Littman 2017).

**Derived guidance for values in [-1,1] (labelled: extrapolation from the above, not a published prescription — no source gives a τ for [-1,1] game values):**

- The relevant yardsticks in the team's trees are (i) the typical sibling gap `Δ` between the best child and the rest, and (ii) the leaf-evaluation noise σ (network error). Soft backup differs materially from hard minimax only when `τ ≳ Δ`; it is ≈ the sibling mean when `τ ≫` the full sibling spread. For a tanh value net on [-1,1], sibling spreads are at most 2 and typically far smaller mid-game, so the interesting sweep is plausibly **τ ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5}** (β ∈ {50, 20, 10, 5, 2}), with τ=0.5 already very soft and τ≥1 essentially averaging. Treat this grid as a starting hypothesis to validate by measuring backup-weight entropy / effective child count on recorded trees, per point 3.
- Use **separate τ for MAX and MIN nodes only if there is a reason** — MCSS allows `T_a ≠ T_b`; symmetry (same τ, negated) is the default in the literature.
- Consider **depth-decaying τ** (larger near leaves, →0 at the root) if root-target fidelity to minimax matters (Baum 1992 rationale; §4).
- Report, alongside playing strength, the **target drift**: mean |soft target − hard minimax value| as a function of depth and τ — this is the quantity the theory says to watch, and no prior paper reports it for this setting.
